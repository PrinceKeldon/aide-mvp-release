## Thread Draft

1.

We’re building AIDE as a personal agent operating layer.

Not just another chatbot.
Not just browser automation.
Not just an email assistant.

AIDE is meant to help one person run their day across:

- memory
- inbox
- calendar
- approvals
- trusted devices
- and eventually other sovereign AIDEs

2.

The core idea is simple:

one agent  
one memory  
many trusted devices  
one approval and action fabric

The goal is continuity.

No matter which model, device, or surface is active, the system should keep the thread.

3.

Today, AIDE already includes:

- central memory across model backends
- multi-model routing across local + cloud
- owner mesh trust, pairing, revoke/restore
- interactive daily brief through `Your Day`
- email read/draft/reply/send flows
- calendar ingestion + conflict detection
- Telegram + web action surfaces

4.

We call the first major layer `Owner Mesh`.

That’s the idea that your AI should work across your own trusted devices as one system, not as disconnected apps.

Your phone, laptop, terminal, and approval surfaces should feel like one operating layer.

5.

That led us to a strong product belief:

approvals are not a popup.

They are a product primitive.

If an agent is going to send, route, write, or collaborate, approval has to be part of the architecture and workflow from the start.

6.

We also built `Your Day` because chat alone is not enough for useful productivity AI.

The right daily surface needs to show:

- what matters
- what is scheduled
- what is conflicting
- what is drafted
- what needs approval
- what you can act on now

7.

The next layer is `M-Peer`.

Plain-language version:

“My AIDE can ask your AIDE for help, but neither of us gives up control.”

That only works if sovereignty stays real.

8.

Which is why one of the most important AIDE decisions is:

another person’s AIDE is not one of your devices.

Peers are not devices.

They need:

- separate trust scopes
- separate invitation flows
- separate task logs
- separate approval logic

9.

We’re building this in public because the interesting part is not just the final product.

It’s the product and systems lessons underneath:

- why trust needs visibility
- why approvals shape UX
- why chat-first is too small
- why model portability matters
- why peers are not devices
