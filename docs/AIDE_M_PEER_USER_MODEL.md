# AIDE M-Peer User Model

Last updated: April 6, 2026

## What is M-Peer?

M-Peer is the part of AIDE that lets your AIDE work with another person’s AIDE.

This is different from Owner Mesh.

Owner Mesh is about:

- your Mac
- your phone
- your trusted owner devices

M-Peer is about:

- your AIDE
- another person’s AIDE
- safe collaboration between two separate people

## What is another AIDE?

Another AIDE is another person’s sovereign agent.

It is not one of your devices.

It belongs to another owner, has its own memory, its own approvals, and its own trust rules.

## What can another AIDE ask mine to do?

Only what you allow.

By default, another AIDE should only be able to request narrow, clearly-scoped work such as:

- research
- summarization
- calendar coordination
- project collaboration

Each peer relationship should use a visible trust scope so you can see what is allowed.

## What can another AIDE see?

By default:

- only what is explicitly included in the request
- no hidden memory
- no automatic access to your wider context

This is important.

Another AIDE should never quietly inherit your memory just because it is trusted for one task.

## What is a trust scope?

A trust scope is the set of rules for what a peer AIDE is allowed to do.

A trust scope answers questions like:

- what kind of tasks can this peer ask for?
- what kind of context can it receive?
- does it need approval for certain actions?
- how restricted is the execution environment?

For most people, trust scopes should appear as simple templates, not technical policy objects.

## What does revoke do?

Revoke ends the peer’s active trust relationship.

After revocation:

- that peer should no longer be allowed to request work
- that peer should no longer receive new collaboration results
- past records may remain for audit, but live trust ends

Revoking a peer should feel immediate and understandable.

## What happens when approval is needed?

If a task or relationship requires approval:

- AIDE should tell you clearly
- AIDE should explain what is being requested
- AIDE should show which peer asked for it
- AIDE should wait for your decision before proceeding

Approvals are part of trust, not a side detail.

## Plain-Language Summary

The simplest way to explain M-Peer is:

**My AIDE can ask your AIDE for help, but neither of us gives up control.**

That is the product promise.
